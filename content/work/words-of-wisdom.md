## The book

*Words of Wisdom: Jamaican Sayings, Their Meanings, and the Lessons of a Lifetime*, by K.L. Craigie — my mother. Fifty-five sayings she grew up hearing in Jamaica, spoken by the grandfather who raised her, by aunts, uncles, and neighbors: fifty-two for the weeks of a year, plus three bonus ones. Each is set down with its plain-English meaning, the patois as she says it where there is one, and a facing page to reflect and write on. Part proverb collection, part guided journal, part family heirloom. It comes in hardcover, paperback, and Kindle.

## What I did

Editing, design, and the doctor bird illustration. The real problem was structural: fifty-five entries, each needing a quoted saying, a meaning, and room to write, had to feel consistent across the whole book without ever feeling like a template stamped fifty-five times. Editing meant shaping her voice into that same repeatable structure without flattening it.

## The doctor bird

I drew the doctor bird by hand first: poses, an eye, a circle for where it would sit, a few variations, and some ideas that went nowhere. Then I redrew and refined the one I kept in Figma and exported it as a vector.

![Left, a notebook page of pen sketches: hummingbirds perched and in flight, an eye, a circle and a small boxy variation. Right, a close pencil sketch of one bird with its wing raised and its long tail streaming down.](/images/work/words-of-wisdom/bird-notebook.webp "The first pass, by hand: a notebook page of poses, variations, and scratch ideas, and the pose I kept.")

![The finished doctor bird mark: a cream disc with a hummingbird cut out of it, wing raised and a long tail streaming down, on the deep green of the cover.](/images/work/words-of-wisdom/bird-mark.webp "Redrawn and refined in Figma, then exported as a vector.
